# OPERATOR: THE UNWRITTEN COAST

## A campaign about the people a city decides it can lose

A ferry reaches Brinewatch carrying eleven exhausted passengers. Their home has vanished from every official map. They remember its streets. They can still hear its bells across the water. The harbor office insists the place never existed.

Your characters become the rescue company willing to go back.

**The Unwritten Coast** is a new, original campaign series: twelve situation-based adventures in three acts, built for a GM and two to four players. It combines an intimate found-family story, strange coastal exploration, faction negotiation and objective-driven tactical encounters. Its central question is practical: **how do you keep people safe when the system protecting them is choosing who counts?**

**Edition 1 — authored 2026-09-11.** Complete campaign manuscript and GM-run play materials. Content has not been playtested or loaded into the game application. Encounter numbers are provisional homebrew. Existing campaigns and live campaign history remain separate.

## What has been rebuilt

| Foundation | This series |
|---|---|

| Party identity | A small rescue and investigation company whose charter the players help write |
| World | Brinewatch, four coastal communities, and the displaced landscape of the Stillwater |
| Central conflict | A storm-defense engine protects the capital by displacing other settlements |
| Relationships | Recurring people with homes, debts, humor, loyalties and changing opinions |
| Adventure structure | Places, evidence and opposing goals; players choose routes and solutions |
| Progression | Rescued people, working shelters, reliable routes, repaired beacons and earned allies |
| Ending | Restore, reform, shut down, evacuate or invent another workable settlement |
| Presentation | Short scene openings, player portraits, discoverable maps, quiet original ambience |

The scope is campaign content. This package does not add an app importer, alter an AI prompt, start recording, select a live campaign or replace the game engine.

## The twelve-adventure series

The order below is the default route, not a requirement to replay solved problems. An adventure usually takes one 2–3-hour session; exploration or a split group can extend it. Resolved objectives stay resolved if the players reach them early.

| Act | Adventure | Main experience | Suggested level |
|---|---|---|---:|
| I — People before paperwork | 01. The Ferry With No Shore | Rescue and first introductions | 3 |
| I | 02. Supper for the Missing | Build a home; decide whom to trust | 3 |
| I | 03. The Auction of Borrowed Roads | Social caper or alternative investigation | 3 |
| I | 04. The Village Under Glass | Explore the lost settlement; choose an evacuation plan | 4 |
| II — The price of shelter | 05. The Saint of Small Repairs | A practical repair and a compromised ally | 4 |
| II | 06. Knives at the Lantern Feast | Character relationships and contested evidence | 4 |
| II | 07. The Weather Below | A hazardous expedition into the defense network | 5 |
| II | 08. A City Votes in the Rain | Public persuasion, mutual aid and policy | 5 |
| III — A coast worth saving | 09. The Night Every Beacon Answers | Coordinate communities under pressure | 6 |
| III | 10. The Crossing Ledger | Return for people left behind | 6 |
| III | 11. The Last Safe Harbor | Execute a chosen plan while rivals act | 7 |
| III | 12. The Coast We Choose | Resolve the storm, ownership and personal promises | 7 |

Advance the whole party after the objectives associated with adventures 3, 6, 8, 10 and 12, reaching levels 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 respectively. These are campaign milestones, not rewards for violence or compliance. Equivalent accomplishments, including early or unconventional solutions, count. Use the group's agreed advancement policy if it differs.

## How to start tonight

1. Send PLAYER_GUIDE.md. Use existing characters if their players want; create new histories only with those players.
2. Spend 15–20 minutes agreeing the rules edition, tone, boundaries, dice policy and company charter. Prepare two to four level-3 characters with their normal starting equipment.
3. The GM reads SESSION_01.md and TABLE_KIT.md's baseline procedures. The series bible answers the larger mystery; players do not need it.
4. Draw the session-one map with six zones or use its grid coordinates. Paper and tokens are sufficient. No new art, music or inference service is required.
5. Read the opening ferry paragraph, stop, and ask what each character does. Complete the rescue before explaining the wider world.

## Why these reference videos matter

This design draws on the techniques established in the [dated video research](../../.planning/research/popular-campaign-video-adaptation-2026-09-11.md): [The Mighty Nein's social introductions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=byva0hOj8CU&t=1800s), [Fantasy High's personal spotlights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_zZxCVBi7-k&t=480s), and [Calamity's concrete, unsettling opening information](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KlIkkeWmVvA&t=870s). Their plots, dialogue, characters and settings are not used here. The numerical pacing targets and campaign mechanics in this pack are original design choices, not findings proven by those videos' popularity.

The first session tests three things: players understand what needs doing, each gets meaningful agency, and the outcome gives them a reason to care about the next session. Later sessions test whether the people and institutions respond consistently to their choices.

## Review status

The package is designed for human GM use with the table's chosen D&D fifth-edition rules. New scenario mechanics and creature profiles are clearly identified as homebrew; no official challenge ratings or encounter-balance certification are claimed. Importing content into the app and running a multiplayer session remain separate work. A campaign outline is never evidence that an event happened to the players.
