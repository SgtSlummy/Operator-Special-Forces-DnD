# The Unwritten Coast — four-session agent playtest

Completed 2026-09-12. This is an accelerated tabletop playtest with four complete adventure arcs, fifteen main decision scenes, one explicit role-coordination exchange and sixteen generated d20 checks. It is not four human-length sessions or an official D&D balance test.

[Full public Discord play record](https://discord.com/channels/1463393482306486387/1546676505780944979/threads/1548380866386858175)

Thread: **The Unwritten Coast · Agent Playtest · Sessions 1–4**. The human opening thread, ending in `1548204446838689882`, was not advanced. No live game, Unity, cloud, campaign database or existing human player state was changed.

## Information boundary and roles

The GM was the parent agent. Two player agents were spawned with `fork_turns: none`, so neither inherited the GM conversation or manuscript context:

- `player_team_a`: Mara Venn, guardian; Ivo Quill, surveyor-mage.
- `player_team_b`: Sable Reed, scout; Brother Tern, medic.

The user then required agents to act only on information visible in Discord. Independent browser reads were attempted. Browser IDs differed by agent; the GM's tab could not be claimed by a player session, and new player tabs required separate login. No player action was taken from those failed reads. The publicly documented alternative was a relay of captured, already-visible playtest posts into the two separate player contexts. No unpublished scene, GM manuscript, hidden outcome or unrevealed map was supplied. Choices were returned independently and attributed in Discord before adjudication.

The relay is an information-handling procedure, not an operating-system security sandbox. Player decisions relied on public scene text, floor descriptions and map captions; the run does not establish independent visual inspection of map geometry by the player agents. The GM displayed actual paired overview/slice exports with the same numbered markers in both views.

## Published rules

A d20 plus strong specialty +4, trained specialty +2 or otherwise +0; target 12 under pressure, 15 hard, 18 extreme. Equal succeeds. One committed helper gives +2 in place of their own action; help does not stack. Each PC starts each session with 6 Resolve and 2 Focus. Spend one Focus before a roll for +2; no rerolls. Between sessions restore two Resolve up to six and reset Focus. At zero Resolve a PC is incapacitated, not automatically dead. Safe actions do not require checks. Stakes and roll order were posted before rolls.

Rolls came from `System.Security.Cryptography.RandomNumberGenerator.GetInt32(1,21)` after declarations. Conditional calibration was generated only after a successful release. Published values were retained, including all six failures. Simultaneous attempts at the same objective were resolved once, not as extra retries; unused Focus was preserved when a character had no separate roll.

## Session 1 — The Ferry With No Shore

### S1.1: secure the crossing

Eleven passengers were aboard a ferry with a fraying spring line and shifting gangplank. Mara committed rope work and Focus, Ivo helped her, Sable independently inspected anchors with Focus, and Tern calmed the vulnerable passengers from the quay. Mara and Sable succeeded; the crossing became safe. Drift and exposure stayed at zero.

### S1.2: the eleventh name

Ten passengers crossed, but Jun was pinned beneath a cargo bench in the lower hold. Mara and Ivo descended; Ivo surveyed a brace before Mara lifted. Tern descended to stabilize Jun, while Sable escorted the ten to the market awning. Ivo's survey succeeded; Tern's treatment failed and Jun's condition worsened to 2/3; Mara's lift succeeded. Jun was freed conscious but unable to walk unaided. The mapped positions were Mara/Ivo/Tern in the lower hold and Sable on the quay before her escort.

### S1.3: a roof, a record, a promise

Vesta offered heated office shelter in exchange for original passenger maps. Nessa consented to copying, not surrender. Both agents independently chose to escort Jun to the open clinic and make a witnessed copy. Their compatible duplicate plans became a shared carry and writer/witness pairing. Vesta supplied a blank sheet and charcoal. No roll was needed. Jun received clinical care; ten slept under the awning. Nessa kept her original.

**Ending:** all eleven rescued; Jun stable and splinted; one witnessed copy; no official shelter bargain. Shared promise: no original map surrendered or shared without its owner's permission. Both players valued safe alternatives and requested clearer carrying and copying-supply information.

## Session 2 — Supper for the Missing

### S2.1: repair a refuge

Ada offered the Net House, materials and tools in exchange for an honest work ledger. Its thirty bunks required a roof repair. Cass offered a heated hall under a one-year travel-control contract. Ivo inspected; Mara attempted bracing; Sable asked Cass about actual terms; Tern asked the eleven refugees about their preferences. Ivo succeeded, Mara failed and lost one Resolve. The west bay closed, leaving twenty safe bunks. Nobody was inside the failed bay. The company accepted only its own work obligations, not forced refugee labor.

### S2.2: eighteen bowls

Twenty-one diners had eighteen portions against an invoice for twenty-four. Ivo investigated the invoice with Focus but failed: rain-smudged evidence could not identify the recipient that night. Sable followed the courier openly to the warehouse gate without alleging theft. Mara and Tern fetched twelve donated blankets. Once warmth was available, all eleven refugees voluntarily chose the Net House and rejected Cass's terms.

### S2.3: a truthful request

Tern requested six replacement portions, with Mara activating her declared helper fallback. Their check failed. Orra recorded the dispute but refused replacement food. The diners agreed to share eighteen portions among twenty-one. Ivo and Sable copied public auction terms: admission free, bids by money or services, no transfer of land or people, occupancy challenges permitted. A notice offered a route to Morrowbank, which Nessa identified as her home. She permitted use of her witnessed copy and offered her own testimony.

**Ending:** independent shelter established; west bay still closed; soup dispute unresolved; route lead obtained. Mara's Resolve recovered between sessions. The debrief clarified that losing a daylight investigative slot prevented examining outdoor marks, not an already-announced safe conversation at a lit gate.

## Session 3 — The Auction of Borrowed Roads

### S3.1: challenge the listing

The registry called Morrowbank uninhabited based on a six-month-old survey. Nessa said she had left three days earlier. Mara and Tern independently petitioned; it became one joint petition led by Tern, with Nessa's voluntary help and Tern's Focus. Mara's Focus remained unspent. The petition succeeded and paused the sale. Ivo's inspection succeeded and found an incomplete return bearing. Sable asked Rook how return worked. Rook confirmed that the route could be shared independently of compass ownership but required calibration against the village bell for safe return.

### S3.2: stop the demonstration

The compass resonated and its glass canopy cracked. Both agents independently chose safe evacuation and the emergency cord rather than risking people to preserve a navigation setting. The first hand pulled the cord; the other did not duplicate the action. Mara and Tern covered exits. Nobody was hurt, but the outbound setting was erased. No roll was invented for the published safe option.

### S3.3: terms of a road

Both teams chose a full day of careful reconstruction. They questioned when the twenty-four-hour loan began and whether surrendering a reusable route would expose the village without its residents' permission. Neither treated Nessa as authorized to consent for everyone. Acceptance remained conditional while questions were answered.

### S3.4: informed departure

Orra agreed that the loan started at departure and the route copy remained jointly sealed until village residents decided whether it could be released. An anonymous occupancy correction could omit names and approach coordinates. The outbound setting was safely reconstructed; return remained explicitly unverified. All four PCs knowingly volunteered for exploration. Nessa freely joined; ten prior refugees remained ashore. Tern's conditional care handoff for Jun was accepted by the clinic and Ada.

**Ending:** sale paused, hazardous demonstration stopped, instrument repaired, conditional access obtained without ownership of residents or land. No guaranteed return was promised. The debrief highlighted overlapping actions and limits on one witness's consent.

## Session 4 — The Village Under Glass

### S4.1: eighteen residents and the base gate

The party counted eighteen residents: fourteen voluntarily wanted to leave after return verification; four wanted to stay. For this playtest the existing Beacon Heart architecture represented the village beacon: 180-foot diameter, 300-foot height, with floors at 0, 100, 200 and 275 feet. The full maintenance layout was publicly disclosed, but current conditions were revealed through scenes.

Ivo inspected the foundation gate. Mara and Sable both initially volunteered to hold the same brake. The GM posted that role conflict before any roll, and Sable explicitly chose to operate the gate while Mara held the brake. Ivo's successful survey enabled the safe two-person release. Tern discussed mobility and supplies with residents. Two evacuees needed steady arms. The party independently counted twenty travelers, below the boat's twenty-four-person capacity.

### S4.2: split floors

Mara worked the counterweight at 100 feet. Ivo, Sable and Tern worked the vane at 200 feet. Their simultaneous vane work became one surveying-led attempt with the one helper; Sable's proposed Focus was not spent on a duplicate roll. Mara failed and lost one Resolve; the counterweight remained caught. The vane succeeded and remained fixed. The state was daylight 3/6, including the failure cost. No one altered the separately labeled storm-shield valve.

### S4.3: controlled release and the crown

Mara used a newly observed rated sling and wedges at 100 feet, with Sable's explicitly conditional help. Ivo went to 275 feet and waited for the all-clear; Tern went to the foundation to arrange willing boarding helpers. Mara succeeded. Only then was Ivo's calibration rolled; it failed because the bell's local sound and delayed echo could not be separated reliably. Return remained unverified and daylight reached 5/6. A visible service plate linked the Morrowbank node to Brinewatch's protection circuit; the players correctly treated that as connection evidence, not proof of motive or culpability.

### S4.4: wait instead of gambling

Both teams chose the published safe full dawn observation sequence, since even a successful rushed measurement would miss that day's tide. They requested actual food counts before sharing provisions. The resident ledger held thirty-six person-day portions: eighteen used that night, leaving fourteen departing portions and four portions for the four staying. The captain's previously uncounted locker was explicitly opened and counted: twelve portions, with six for the six visitors that night and six for their return. This stock was disclosed as newly counted, not treated as previously published knowledge. Village residents consented to using a six-person cloth shelter and their bedding for the five people beyond Net House capacity. Route disclosure remained sealed pending a village meeting.

### S4.5: verified return and voluntary relocation

The complete dawn sequence verified return without a roll, as the agreed safe option costing a night and food. Elian and Iset witnessed it. All twenty travelers boarded with the two willing helpers, carrying only permitted supplies. The one-hour crossing completed at loan hour twenty-two, and Orra acknowledged timely compass return and sealed-copy conditions. Ada approved placing the six-person shelter in the east yard. Twenty bunks plus five sheltered places housed all twenty-five refugees.

Both agents chose an anonymous factual correction: eighteen residents were counted, fourteen chose to travel to Brinewatch, four chose to stay. No names, approach coordinates or reusable route instructions were published. The shield remained unchanged.

**Ending:** fourteen additional people relocated safely; the four who stayed retained agency and one day of food. Resupply was discussed but not performed or guaranteed. The damaged west bay and six-meal dispute remained open. No fifth session was simulated.

## Findings and limits

What worked: explicit stakes; failure consequences that persisted; safe actions with meaningful time or resource costs; permission before taking originals or publishing access; different floor assignments with shared marker identities; conditional rolls after prerequisite success; a population and shelter ledger that balanced.

What needs improvement: establish boat provisions before departure; label how a failed time slot limits subsequent actions; use a brief public planning phase to resolve duplicated roles; establish cargo, copying materials and carrying assistance early; distinguish a witnessed claim from verified evidence.

This playtest used the campaign README's first four adventure titles and central premise, with improvised NPC names and encounter details. It did not validate every encounter in the full manuscript, combat balance, fog-of-war access controls, geometry/pathfinding, actual human group dynamics, or Unity integration. No code functionality was changed. The existing human campaign is still waiting for its players' first actions.
