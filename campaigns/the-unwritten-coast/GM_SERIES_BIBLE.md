# The Unwritten Coast — GM series bible

**GM ONLY.** These are prepared setting facts and possible developments for a new campaign. They do not describe events already played. Use [EPISODES.md](EPISODES.md), [SESSION_01.md](SESSION_01.md) and [TABLE_KIT.md](TABLE_KIT.md) at the table.

## The truth, in one page

The coast faces the Long Gale, a severe natural storm front. The five-beacon network once distributed storm energy across the region. Years of deferred maintenance left only Brinewatch's Crown Beacon fully reliable.

Chief Engineer **Wren Sol** built a temporary alternative: the **Continuance Engine**, housed below the Crown Beacon. It diverts the most dangerous storm energy into a parallel coastal space called the **Stillwater**. It does so by shifting a connected settlement into that space with its inhabitants and structures intact.

**Provost Elian Rook** authorized the first displacement: Bellwether. He believes protecting the capital's population justifies the emergency measure. He has concealed the affected people to prevent panic and to keep his coalition in office. He plans to displace Mossmere next if the original problem remains unsolved.

Official maps are magically linked to beacon survey plates. When the engine moves a settlement, linked maps show its former footprint as water. Ordinary memories, unlinked sketches, keepsakes and personal records do not change. Rook exploits the altered official maps as bureaucratic cover. He does not control everyone's perception.

Bellwether's residents are alive. Eleven escaped aboard a ferry using a survey lantern that briefly identifies a route through the boundary. The lantern carried by **Nessa Vale** is a practical navigation tool; it does not contain anyone's soul and does not require a destined character.

The safe long-term alternative is to restore distributed defense. It needs a working Crown Beacon, at least two of the four outer beacons, a calibrated bypass and operators who can coordinate them. This spreads the storm load across uninhabited tidal basins and prepared waterfront spillways. It can restore displaced settlements after the peak front passes, but causes property damage and temporary port closure. Evacuation reduces casualties. Rook dislikes the political and financial costs; Wren initially doubts that enough people can be organized in time.

The PCs can prove, change or reject those plans. No prophecy chooses the company. No NPC secretly owns a player character's history. The final threat is a storm and a damaged institution, not a requirement to defeat a boss in combat.

## The coast and travel

Brinewatch is the home base. The Crooked Oar sits beside its lower quay. Lowmarket is a district inside Brinewatch, not a sixth beacon settlement.

| Place | Public character | Beacon condition at start | Useful travel estimate from Brinewatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brinewatch / Crown Beacon | Harbor, workshops, bureaucracy, crowded refuge | Working, engine attached | Local travel, 15–45 minutes |
| Bellwether | Ferry town, rope-makers and orchard terraces | Working plate, settlement displaced | Former crossing 1 hour; Stillwater access needs a route |
| Mossmere | Marsh growers, reed boats, community repair yards | Usable tower, cracked conductor | 3 hours by shallow-draft boat |
| Glasshaven | Merchants, canals, survey archives | Intact apparatus, deliberately disconnected | 5 hours by coastal vessel |
| Redwake | Quarry port and exposed cliff paths | Sound conductor, broken rotating mount | 6 hours by coastal vessel |
| Old Observatory | Survey station above Brinewatch | Test equipment only; not a sixth defense beacon | 2 hours on foot |
| Stormvault | Maintenance chambers beneath the Crown | Connected to the same Crown Beacon | 30 minutes from the quay by known service routes |

Travel times assume ordinary conditions and a usable route. Rest, provisions, weather and injuries use the table's chosen rules. Do not advance campaign progress tracks merely because a session ended. Establish elapsed time and current hazards first.

A world sketch can place Redwake northwest, Glasshaven west, Bellwether across the central bay, Mossmere southeast and Brinewatch on the southern shore. This is a navigational relationship map, not a surveyed tactical grid.

## Stillwater: consistent rules for an impossible place

1. People are physically present and conscious. Time, hunger, aging and recovery proceed normally. Stillwater is not a loophole for unlimited rest while the outside world is paused.
2. Displacement affects a survey-defined footprint. It does not follow a name spoken by a player or erase a distant relative. There are no surprise PC memory edits.
3. A calibrated route can be crossed at dawn or dusk, each window lasting one hour. The party sees the window closing as lantern beams narrow. A crossing normally takes ten minutes. The GM tracks expedition time openly.
4. A survey lantern plus a route chart identifies a stable crossing. A lantern alone points toward one; it does not explain how to traverse it. A chart alone can guide a crossing using the Observatory's replacement instrument. Neither is unique or irreplaceable.
5. A departing ferry wakes harmless echoes of former sounds and movements. Echoes can reveal past physical activity; they cannot predict choices or provide omniscient truth.
6. Normal character abilities work according to the adopted rules. Consider a spell's actual range, destination and requirements. The campaign adds no blanket ban on divination, flight, teleportation, healing or escape. If an ability bypasses a planned obstacle, award the advantage and move on.
7. When a crossing window closes, travelers can wait safely in a prepared refuge if they have arranged one. The next window opens normally unless an established engine event changes it. Explain the evidence of any such change before resolving the danger.
8. Defeating a guardian or official does not reverse displacement. Moving residents through a route and restoring land through the engine are different tasks.

## Four factions, each with something useful

## A home worth returning to

The Crooked Oar has a leaking common room, a stove, six shared bunks and a locked store cupboard. After the first rescue Mara offers workspace in exchange for help arranging supplies and listening to residents' needs. Ownership of the boardinghouse stays with Mara unless explicitly negotiated.

The PCs name their company and choose one useful improvement after each act: a dry chart room, repair bench, infirmary corner, improved kitchen, accessible landing or guest room. Each improvement should be attached to labor and resources secured in play. These are narrative facilities, not extra healing or crafting rules unless the GM adopts a specific mechanic.

At a return visit, use one small interaction: a new recipe, an argument about shoes, a repaired toy, an unanswered letter or a resident asking for privacy. Offer a character a chance to speak. Do not require a speech, a romantic subplot or a tragic reveal.

## The antagonists move when the situation gives them a reason

Use publicly signaled institutional actions. Rook begins with an inspection request. If challenged with credible evidence, he offers a private hearing. If evidence threatens his position and the company refuses compromise, he may seek a lawful detention order or restrict official resources. He knows only what his people learned.

Rook is responsible for the original decision. He is not responsible for every accident, storm or unfriendly NPC. Wren participated and has relevant responsibility too; helping later does not erase that history.

If exposed early, Rook can lose authority before adventure 8. His replacement still faces the engineering problem. If killed, the engine remains and the council elects an interim provost. If supported, the company must still decide who bears the costs. If convinced early, adventures 6–11 become public coordination and repairs with political opponents taking other roles. Do not restore his secrecy or villainy to keep the outline intact.

## Three fronts and earned breathing space

Track **Storm Pressure 0–6**, **Shelter Strain 0–4** and **Official Scrutiny 0–4** as defined in TABLE_KIT.md. Start all at 0. Each advance needs a recorded fictional trigger and an observable sign. Do not advance for real-world conversation, missed attendance, roleplaying slowly or using a pause tool.

Storm 6 means the Long Gale arrives, not that everyone dies or the campaign automatically ends. Use the chosen emergency response and the people/assets actually prepared. If it arrives early, fold later investigations into post-storm recovery. If the PCs prepare early, give them the benefit instead of conjuring an extra storm.

Shelter 4 means a specific shortage needs a response; name the resource and who is affected. Scrutiny 4 means a public confrontation or hearing, not loss of character control. Both can be reduced by concrete remedies.

## Endings the players can actually reach

Explain known requirements and likely consequences before the final decision. These are prepared possibilities, not a menu that forbids a fourth solution.

| Plan | What makes it viable | Costs and result | If a requirement is missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared coast | Crown + two outer beacons functioning; calibrated bypass; willing operators | Engine displacements end. After the peak front, displaced land returns over one tide. Prepared spillways absorb damage; port trade closes during repair | Repair a third beacon, recruit another crew, or use temporary masts for one missing outer beacon with a full day of labor and Consortium/municipal supplies |
| Evacuate and release | Stable crossings, adequate boats/shelter, Crown shutoff access | People leave Stillwater before shutdown. Unprotected buildings and port property face the storm. Land returns after the engine stops; the coast must rebuild defenses | Stagger trips, open civic buildings, commandeer or negotiate more capacity under the table's decisions; every shortfall is visible before committing |
| Controlled continuation | Crown remains staffed; the next displacement is cancelled; residents freely choose temporary refuge plans | Temporary shelter in Stillwater can continue. It requires supply routes, public oversight, a disclosed schedule and a later restoration project | Without informed agreement and reliable supplies it is Rook's original injustice, not a secretly good outcome; show the opposition and immediate rescue options |
| A player-created plan | GM tests its assumptions against the established world and actual character abilities | Resolve demonstrated benefits and costs; provide a pilot test when feasible | Ask for missing information or resources, not conformity to a prewritten ending |

No solution demands a PC death, erased character, involuntary transformation or compulsory loss of a loved one. A player may propose a costly sacrifice; discuss its actual mechanical effect rather than treating drama as unlimited authority.

When the storm is addressed, finish three things at the table: who controls the beacons; what happens to the residents and company promises; and what each character chooses to do next. Resolve the present campaign before offering future trouble.

## Continuation after the twelve-adventure series

Future series are optional seeds, not unfinished requirements for this one. A restored coast may ask the company to negotiate with communities beyond its old maps. An evacuated coast may need a rebuilding season. A temporary-refuge ending may lead to a public engineering expedition. Use the ending actually earned; do not reset the world to the opening premise.

## Running principles

Give observable information, receive intentions, clarify only what changes the ruling, apply the adopted rules, record the result, then offer the next decision. Keep GM explanations separate from shared maps and recaps. AI suggestions do not choose actions, invent successful outcomes, alter consent or change authoritative state. A human GM can run this entire pack with paper, dice and character sheets.
