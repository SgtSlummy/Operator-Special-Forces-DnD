# Act I — People before paperwork

**GM ONLY.** Adventures 1–4. Suggested starting level 3; advance to 4 after obtaining a crossing route or an equivalent major accomplishment. Use the original profiles and scenario-check procedure in [TABLE_KIT.md](TABLE_KIT.md). DCs are scenario suggestions, not restrictions on effective spells or good plans.

## 01. The Ferry With No Shore

**Purpose:** introduce people through urgent cooperation and establish the campaign mystery. The full runnable version, including coordinates, rescue steps and aftermath, is in [SESSION_01.md](SESSION_01.md).

**Opening:** a ferry carrying eleven Bellwether residents strikes the quay. One mooring has broken; the gangway is shifting. Nessa's survey lantern points toward a town missing from the official map.

**Places:** the ferry deck, a service pontoon beneath the quay, and the harbor inspection shelter. The ferry can be secured from shore or from its deck. Both routes remain visible.

**People:** Nessa tries to keep passengers together. Holt wants a safe landing and initially assumes a navigation error. Mara brings blankets. None attacks merely because the PCs ask a question.

**Playable decisions:** secure the boat, board and guide passengers, call for dockworkers, organize evacuation through the service pontoon, or improvise. A player can watch the crowd or equipment while another helps. The GM explains the practical cost of leaving a task unfinished before moving the situation forward.

**Evidence:** residents can describe their homes; a passenger's unlinked hand-drawn map shows Bellwether; the lantern bears a Crown survey-works maker's plate. All are available without a single gatekeeping check. The ferry's damaged register gives a later route to Ivo.

**Opposition:** physical instability and distrust, not a compulsory attack. If characters initiate a confrontation, Holt uses the Quay Constable profile and seeks separation and a hearing. Do not combine an escalating rescue hazard with a punitive combat merely to increase difficulty.

**Aftermath:** record who was helped, the ferry's condition, possession of the lantern and any promises. The eleven passengers survive unless actual play and agreed stakes establish otherwise. A poor rescue can cost equipment or delay, without erasing every lead. Mara offers a table at the Crooked Oar. If declined, Holt's shelter or the Ferrymen's warehouse can serve.

**Next invitation:** residents want somewhere safe tonight and a way to contact those left in Bellwether. Players decide which need to address first.

## 02. Supper for the Missing

**Opening:** Mara sets twelve bowls on the table, counts eleven new guests and leaves the last bowl in place. A resident asks where they can find work tomorrow.

**Objective:** secure a sustainable refuge and agree how the company operates. This is a social and practical adventure, not a cutscene between fights.

**Locations:** the Crooked Oar's crowded kitchen; a Wardens' relief warehouse; the open noticeboard on Netmakers' Lane. Spend the first ten minutes inviting one everyday interaction from each PC. Then let them divide the practical jobs.

**People and moves:** Mara will offer six bunks and floor space for the first night without payment. Orrin can lend cots if the company arranges safe transport. Holt can authorize rations after learning how many people need them. A warehouse clerk wants a named recipient to avoid duplicate claims; showing the actual residents or obtaining Holt's endorsement settles this without persuasion.

**Routes:** negotiate civic relief; recruit neighbors to host residents; exchange a day's useful repair work for supplies; pay from the company grant; or combine these. A difficult request made without supporting evidence may use DC 13 appropriate social interaction. An achievable practical job with tools and sufficient time succeeds; roll only when haste or danger creates meaningful uncertainty.

**Complication:** the relief warehouse lists Bellwether as an invalid destination. It can still supply people currently standing in Brinewatch. Ivo is embarrassed by this distinction and will explain it. He will not alter records to invent an outcome, but he can make a signed correction and keep a copy.

**Evidence:** Ivo has an unlinked tax-copy book; the noticeboard holds old ferry advertisements; several tradespeople remember Bellwether customers. These independently establish that the town is not a collective delusion.

**Aftermath:** secure food and a safe place for a day and Shelter Strain stays at 0; an unresolved shortage advances it only after that day actually passes. The company chooses a name, three charter lines and a keeper for shared funds. Record each resident's preferred arrangement. They are not party property.

**Next invitation:** Ivo identifies an auction of survey routes; Orrin knows a retired survey pilot; Mara heard the Observatory still has working instruments. Any can lead toward adventure 3's accomplishment.

## 03. The Auction of Borrowed Roads

**Opening:** a bidder offers a silk purse for a strip of map no wider than a finger. The strip shows a road ending in the middle of the sea.

**Objective:** obtain a usable, calibrated crossing route. The auction is the default venue, not the only route to success.

**Locations:** Tamsin's public preview gallery; a private bidding room; the service courtyard where deliveries are checked. A dry cloak and ordinary introduction gain entry to the preview. Tamsin will lend the party a bidding token against a specific future favor; establish that favor before they agree.

**Prepared facts:** the sale includes three useful lots: a Bellwether crossing chart, a copied diversion order and an obsolete rotating-mount plan useful at Redwake. A route chart costs 40 gp at the opening bid; Tamsin will sell access to copy it for 15 gp or a negotiated equivalent. Bidding is not a test of real player wealth or performance.

**Routes:** buy or copy the chart; persuade its surveyor to publish it; trade reliable first-hand evidence; or attempt theft and accept the consequences. If the company avoids the auction, Ivo's archived soundings plus a meeting with Orrin's retired pilot produce the same route after half a day. The Observatory offers a third method: a one-day survey using its instrument.

**Opposition:** two hired Quay Constable-profile guards protect property and call for help; they do not attack honest bidders. Stealth or forgery may use DC 13 if the method leaves uncertainty. Failure means discovery or loss of access to that room, not destruction of every route source.

**Evidence:** the chart names dawn and dusk crossings; the diversion order carries Rook's signature; the preview demonstration makes a linked map change while an ordinary charcoal copy stays unchanged. Each can be inspected or independently corroborated later.

**Personal beat:** let a PC identify something small they would bid for purely because they like it. A friend or rival may ask why. There is no concealed penalty for enjoying the scene.

**Aftermath:** record who owns or has copied each lot, debts to Tamsin, public versus private evidence and any lawful complaints. Obtaining a viable crossing route earns the first milestone regardless of method. Do not require the signature evidence to move forward.

**Next invitation:** Nessa asks for a return trip at the next crossing window. Players choose supplies, companions and objectives.

## 04. The Village Under Glass

**Opening:** the lantern beam finds cobblestones beneath apparently empty water. A man on the other side is sweeping his doorstep. He stops when he sees the ferry.

**Objective:** contact Bellwether, verify residents' needs and choose a rescue or supply plan. Arrive through any established route. Crossing takes ten minutes within the known one-hour dawn/dusk window.

**Locations:** the ferry square, a community kitchen in the schoolhouse and the beacon terrace. The schoolhouse is a safe overnight refuge. Announce the remaining crossing-window time whenever travel or a significant task consumes it.

**People and moves:** local organizer Ada Fen has counted sixty residents still present. Forty want the earliest practical passage; twenty initially prefer to stay if supplied. These preferences can change after honest discussion. No one needs to be persuaded that their own town exists. Ada wants accurate information and a passenger plan; she does not automatically appoint the PCs governors.

**Routes:** inspect the beacon first; organize immediate ferry trips; remain for the next window to survey and repair; or return with a supply list. The ferry safely carries twenty passengers plus crew per crossing. Within one window it can make one round trip with loading, safely evacuating twenty people; further trips occur at the next window. Other boats or relevant magic can increase capacity through their actual limits.

**Obstacle:** a maintenance Sentinel guards the beacon's moving machinery. It gives an audible warning before engaging. Nessa's legitimate survey tag, safe shutdown of the outer gate or a DC 13 appropriate technical check can secure access. Combat is optional; use one Light Sentinel for two PCs, or the early-party guidance for larger groups. Disabling this guardian does not repair the whole beacon.

**Evidence:** residents saw survey crews the day before displacement; the terrace register records an intentional Crown command; an intact load plate shows the other four beacon names. Ada has a paper copy of the register in case the machinery is damaged.

**Aftermath:** record the number leaving, who stays by choice, food deliveries and the promised return window. A missed window means waiting in the prepared refuge, not unavoidable drowning. Rest and supplies remain real. The remaining residents can organize without the PCs.

**Next invitation:** the load plate points to Mossmere's failing conductor; Ada asks why emergency repairs there were cancelled. Rook's administration requests a meeting if the company has made its evidence public. Neither request overrides the players' plan.
