# Act III — A coast worth saving

**GM ONLY.** Adventures 9–12. Usually levels 6–7; advance to 7 after adventure 10's rescue/return accomplishment and to 8 when the campaign concludes. Read the current state before preparing. Earlier repairs, evacuations, deaths, agreements and changes of government stay true.

## 09. The Night Every Beacon Answers

**Opening:** from the Crooked Oar's roof, the company sees a distant beacon flash twice. Orrin checks the reply schedule and says, “That one wasn't supposed to be working yet.”

**Objective:** assemble the actual capacity needed for the chosen plan. Distributed defense needs Crown plus at least two working outer beacons with willing operators. An evacuation plan instead needs crossings, sufficient transport, safe landing space and shelter. Do not force the party to complete repairs it has deliberately chosen not to use.

**Locations and prepared jobs:**

- **Mossmere:** replace the cracked conductor sleeve and calibrate it, using adventure 5's established repair. If already done, obtain or confirm the operators' agreement; do not break it again.
- **Glasshaven:** its apparatus is intact but disconnected under a commercial dispute. Tamsin can negotiate a public payment schedule, the town can authorize reconnection, or workers can independently choose to reconnect. A threat does not produce durable consent without consequences.
- **Redwake:** lift and reset the rotating mount. The quarry crane plus a crew and six hours completes the operation. Without the crane, a stable scaffolding plan and a full day of labor works. Under danger, a DC 15 appropriate tools or Strength check may resolve the uncertain lift; failure pauses work and damages replaceable bracing.
- **Bellwether:** the beacon works in Stillwater but cannot count as an outer-world defense beacon until the town returns. It is useful for crossings and later restoration, not a hidden shortcut that contradicts the load diagram.

**Routes:** travel with the company, delegate clearly bounded work to willing crews, or split the party with agreed spotlight rotation. Delegated work requires actual personnel, materials, travel time and an exit plan. Resolve established competence honestly; do not roll secretly until a crew fails just to create a rescue.

**Opposition:** a storm-damaged Sentinel may guard Redwake's crane shed. Announce it before a fight. A valid service order, isolated power or a clear route around the shed can solve access. Use one Heavy Sentinel for two level-6 PCs only if the table wants a demanding optional encounter and resources are healthy; the Standard profile is the conservative default. More enemies are not the reward for a clever repair plan.

**Evacuation alternative:** Orrin can recruit one additional twenty-passenger vessel per fulfilled commitment: wages and supplies, a trustworthy pilot, a landing slot and shelter for the passengers. Resolve each commitment in play; no endless free fleet appears.

**Aftermath:** record every beacon's physical condition separately from its operators' consent. Record boats, safe passenger capacity and expected crossing windows. A temporary mast can replace one missing outer beacon after a full day of labor and secured industrial supplies; it imposes later repair costs, not a surprise sacrifice.

**Next invitation:** Nessa asks the company to settle the promises still outstanding across the boundary before anyone commits to a final engine change.

## 10. The Crossing Ledger

**Opening:** Ada lays a passenger list beside a list of people choosing to remain. “Read both,” she says. “Everyone gets to be a person, not a number.”

**Objective:** resolve the remaining Bellwether rescue, supply and return commitments. Start from the recorded counts, not the initial sixty. If everyone has already left, the operation becomes a voluntary retrieval of tools, keepsakes or civic records and an inspection of the return site.

**Locations:** Bellwether schoolhouse, the orchard road and the beacon landing. The orchard contains bulky food stores and a spare survey instrument. A damaged handcart can be repaired or unloaded. These are useful choices about limited time and capacity, not a mandatory choice between two lives.

**People and moves:** Ada wants everyone to hear the likely consequences of each plan. Nessa wants a final reliable count and is willing to stay another window if needed. Residents may choose to leave, remain temporarily or accompany a relative. Give them understandable information; do not manufacture consensus by one persuasion roll.

**Routes:** make additional scheduled trips, borrow the second vessel earned earlier, establish a stocked refuge for a later window, use relevant character abilities, or return after obtaining capacity. The known one-hour crossing windows and ferry load limits still apply. Tell the players what can fit before they allocate it.

**Complication:** a reflected harbor sound makes one uncalibrated route appear inviting. The stable chart and survey lantern distinguish it without a roll when used together. If characters rush down the unverified route, describe mismatched landmarks and let them turn back before imposing a danger. A DC 15 Navigation-related check can resolve uncertainty if the instruments were lost; failure costs time and returns them to the last landmark, not automatic death.

**Evidence:** Ada's copied register corroborates the original displacement order. The spare survey instrument provides a fallback if Nessa's lantern is gone. A beacon service plaque explains that full shutdown returns land over one tide and should follow evacuation of exposed structures.

**Personal beat:** each PC can choose one promise to resolve, renegotiate or honestly admit they cannot yet fulfill. The GM does not invent an unconsented relative trapped in a house to force emotional stakes.

**Aftermath:** total the residents still in Stillwater, their stated preferences, supplies and the next expected contact. Make the ledger exact. The milestone is addressing the people and obligations meaningfully, including a credible continuing-supply plan—not forcing everyone to evacuate against their wishes.

**Next invitation:** the forecast and actual Storm Pressure indicate what time remains. The company prepares its chosen operation at Crown.

## 11. The Last Safe Harbor

**Dungeon map:** Crown access is R14 in [The Undertow Works](DUNGEON_ATLAS.md). The final operation can use R18's stations, R15's basins and R16's surface relay. Preserve routes and machinery conditions established on earlier visits.

**Opening:** the harbor cranes stop turning as the first long gust reaches the quay. At the Crooked Oar, the company unfolds the plan it has spent the campaign making possible.

**Objective:** put that plan into position. This is a readiness-and-action adventure with visible tradeoffs, not an automatic assault on a fortress.

**Locations:** Crown's control gallery, the waterfront spillways and the civic shelter district. The party identifies who works at each place, how messages travel, what starts the operation and what causes a safe abort. Players can change the plan when they see a missing resource.

**Three prepared problems:** a jammed spillway lever needs repair or a safe alternative channel; a crew needs relief before another shift; an official or commercial claimant disputes access. Use only problems not already solved by the party's previous work. A working crew and tools can fix the lever with time. Resting or replacing people resolves fatigue through the adopted rules. Evidence, a hearing, negotiated payment or independent authority can resolve access.

**Rook's current move:** if still in power and opposed, he offers immediate city protection in return for allowing Mossmere's displacement. If publicly removed, he can argue at a hearing or comply with lawful restrictions; he does not magically regain guards. If persuaded earlier, he supplies resources while defending his past conduct. If dead, the interim administration handles the same logistics without resurrecting his role.

**Routes:** escorted entry, a negotiated temporary control agreement, an independently staffed shutdown or unauthorized intervention with known consequences. If a confrontation occurs, state each side's objective and exit condition. NPCs can surrender, withdraw or cooperate; defeating every opponent is not required to operate equipment.

**Optional encounter:** one Heavy Sentinel guards the gallery under conflicting authorization. The party can show a valid transferred order, isolate its circuit or fight. For two PCs do not add guards to its active turn order. If the company already resolved authorization and maintenance, omit it entirely. Preserve spell and feature effectiveness.

**Readiness check:** before moving to the finale, show the actual beacon status, willing crews, calibrated bypass/shutdown notes, remaining Stillwater residents, transport, shelter and evacuation of flood zones. Separate a known shortfall from an unknown outcome. Offer a practical remedy or a transparently costlier alternative.

**Aftermath:** no point of no return is crossed by narration alone. Ask for the party's decision to begin, delay, change or abandon an operation. Storm Pressure advances only from its established weather trigger. If the front already arrived, adapt the same problems to stabilization and relief.

## 12. The Coast We Choose

**Opening:** five signal positions stand on the control table. Which are lit depends on the work the company and its allies actually completed. Outside, the wind reaches the shutters.

**Objective:** resolve the Long Gale response, the displaced communities and the political settlement. Use the plan requirements in [GM_SERIES_BIBLE.md](GM_SERIES_BIBLE.md). Describe likely costs before the players commit.

**Run the operation in three phases:**

1. **People:** confirm evacuation, staffed refuges or freely chosen temporary residence. A shortfall becomes a concrete rescue or transport task. Never bury a casualty inside an abstract failed check.
2. **Power:** carry out the selected bypass, shutoff or controlled operation. Prepared instructions plus trained crews succeed at routine work. Roll only for a distinct uncertainty created by an established complication. A failed check first changes position, timing, equipment or access according to the stated stakes; it does not arbitrarily cancel all prior successes.
3. **Return:** keep communication open through the peak front and the following tide. Resolve the residents and property actually exposed. Repaired beacons, prepared spillways and organized crews reduce costs. Missing preparation has visible consequences and opportunities for further action.

**Shared-coast result:** with Crown, two outer beacons, calibration and operators, the bypass carries the load into prepared uninhabited basins. Land returns after the peak front over one tide. Waterfront property may need repair and trade pauses. The company can further limit damage through the work already done.

**Evacuation-and-release result:** with safe crossings and shelter, residents reach the outer world before shutdown. The port and empty buildings face the storm without full protection; people and movable property can be prioritized. Land returns after the engine stops over one tide. Rebuilding becomes the settlement's chosen future.

**Controlled-continuation result:** stopping the next forced displacement while maintaining supply and informed consent gives a temporary refuge arrangement. Residents choose representatives, a review date and a restoration project. If consent or supplies are absent, openly acknowledge that the harm remains unresolved and let the players continue responding to it.

**Other plans:** test them against established physics and actual abilities. A demonstrated innovation can work. Do not invent a new cosmic prohibition because it avoids the prepared dilemma.

**Settle responsibility:** hear affected residents, determine beacon ownership and access, and decide what process addresses Rook's and Wren's actions. A rescue success is not automatic absolution; a criminal finding is not the GM's mandatory ending. Let the company state its view and the wider community have a voice.

**Personal epilogues:** ask each player what their character does at the next meal, which promise they keep or renegotiate, and whether they remain with the company. Then show one concrete changed place—a repaired landing, an open route, a staffed refuge or a town returning to its neighbors.

**Final record:** record the engine mode, every community's status, remaining obligations and each player's chosen epilogue. Award the concluding milestone. End on the world they made. An optional future-series seed comes only after this ending is complete.
