# Session 01 — The Ferry With No Shore

**GM ONLY.** A complete opening session for two to four level-3 characters, usually 2–3 hours including the table agreement. A shorter 60–90-minute session can finish after the first Crooked Oar conversation. Use [TABLE_KIT.md](TABLE_KIT.md) for homebrew profiles, hazard tracking, handouts and the save sheet.

## Five-minute GM preparation

Read the truth summary in GM_SERIES_BIBLE.md, but do not deliver it to the players. Put eleven passenger markers and two crew markers on the ferry. There are sixty other Bellwether residents still in Stillwater. No one on the ferry is a disguised compulsory villain.

Prepare H1, a blank player note page and the six-zone map below. Nessa has the survey lantern. Holt arrives during the landing; Mara comes with blankets. Use the Quay Constable profile only if an actual confrontation calls for it. No combat is required to complete the adventure.

Place each character on the quay in a location of their choice. Do not spend the opening inventing lengthy shared history. One player-approved connection per character is enough.

## Table agreement — 15–20 minutes

Confirm the rules edition and complete character sheets. Agree dice handling, desired danger, how pauses work and any boundaries around water peril, injury or displacement. If drowning is unwelcome, frame the threat as grounding, damaged possessions and being stranded; all core clues and choices still work.

Ask each player what useful thing their character does when someone needs help. Let them name one small comfort and one relationship with another PC, with both players' agreement. Skip or postpone any question that feels burdensome. A veteran character can be visiting this new setting without losing their existing history.

## Opening read-aloud — stop after this paragraph

> The ferry strikes the quay hard enough to scatter mugs from a nearby stall. Eleven passengers crowd its deck. A woman holds a lantern in both hands, its light pointing sideways across the bay.
>
> “Please,” she calls. “The rest of them are still there.”
>
> A mooring rope snaps. The gangway slides. On the harbor map behind you, the ferry's home port has become a patch of empty water.

Ask: **“Where are you standing, and what do you do?”** Get an intention from every player before resolving a long chain of actions. Ordinary clarification does not consume rescue time.

Suggested starting squares are E3 and H3 for two PCs; add C3 and J3 for more. Players may choose other visible quay positions.

| Zone | What is visible | Useful interactions |
|---|---|---|
| A. Warehouse, A1–D2 | Rope coils, a handcart, two dockworkers | Ask for help; obtain a spare line or blanket |
| B. Quay, row 3 | Strong bollards, scattered mugs, worried bystanders | Secure lines, organize movement, direct help |
| C. Gangway, G4–G5 | A sliding plank bridge, ten feet long | Stabilize, cross with suitable precautions, withdraw to another route |
| D. Ferry, E6–J8 | Eleven passengers, two crew, Nessa's light, a loose crate | Help people, tie to a cleat, inspect the ticket/register |
| E. Pontoon, K4–L6 | Low stable landing beside the ferry | Side exit from J6 to K6; cargo can obstruct it if drift worsens |
| F. Inspection shelter, H1–L2 | A dry bench, linked official map, signaling bell | First aid, witness statements, compare records, call harbor launch |

An exposed mudbank lies 30 ft east of the ferry's initial edge, beyond the map. If the ferry grounds there, extend the map or use a new zone; do not pretend the initial gangway still reaches it. The harbor launch can reach it in five minutes after a clear signal and an available crew.

Nessa begins at F7. One crew member is at G6 and the other at I8. Keep civilians in groups for description, but track all eleven people when counting an evacuation. Do not give thirteen NPCs full turns unless combat actually requires it.

## Scene 1 — Secure a way off

**Prepared facts:** the hull is afloat and not sinking. The remaining mooring can hold while a replacement is secured, provided people do not intentionally release it. A crate is making the deck awkward; it does not contain a hidden monster. A service pontoon provides an alternate landing. The ferry's engine and sail are not useful within this tight berth.

**The immediate objective is to establish one stable landing, then move the passengers to safety.** The players may secure the boat first, arrange the pontoon route or use another effective method. It is not necessary to complete every task in the following table.

| Approach | Resolution when conditions are adequate | If rushed or opposed | Stated cost of failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carry a spare line to the crew and tie off | Proper line, reachable cleat, cooperation and time succeed | DC 10 appropriate physical/tool check only if the ferry is already drifting | Line misses or slips; try from a new position or use the pontoon; one justified instability step |
| Stabilize the gangway | Two helpers and a secured boat make a safe crossing | A lone rushed effort may use DC 13 Strength (Athletics) | Plank slips away from the berth; use pontoon or another boarding method; no automatic fall |
| Move loose cargo from pontoon/deck | Enough hands and time succeed | DC 10 Athletics for a hurried heavy lift | Delay or lost crate; people can use another route |
| Organize dockworker assistance | A clear request and a practical assignment succeed | Roll only for an actual disputed request, not ordinary emergency help | Workers ask for clarification or a different task; no automatic hostility |
| Calm a frightened passenger | A safe route and patient explanation usually suffice | DC 10 appropriate interaction only if a specific competing fear makes it uncertain | They wait with a helper while others go first |
| Use a spell, feature or other invention | Apply its actual text and the declared method | Use the adopted rules | Explain any scope limit before the player spends the resource |

Use the local ferry-instability tracker in TABLE_KIT.md. It advances for its stated drift events, not for each roll or each turn. Once a line or landing is safe, stop drift. If players choose to let the ferry ground and call the launch, that is a viable slower solution with cargo and repair costs.

**Unexpected falls:** explain edge risk before a knowingly risky action. If someone actually falls, use the table's swimming, movement and rescue rules with the visible pontoon or a thrown line available. The ferry is not secretly in an endless lethal current. Ordinary rescue assistance works. Adjust framing to the agreed table boundaries.

**First character spotlight:** after a player commits to helping, ask what their teammate notices about the way they work. Let the player supply the detail. Do not pause a vital action for a forced monologue.

## Scene 2 — People and evidence

Once the landing is safe, move the civilians to the inspection shelter or another place the players choose. Allow about ten fictional minutes for all eleven passengers to disembark carefully. If drift or combat changed access, use the actual situation.

**Nessa:** a tired courier, protective but not evasive about the central problem. She says there are sixty people still in Bellwether and asks for a return trip when possible. She does not know the engine's political history. Asked what happened, she describes ordinary streets surrounded by unfamiliar still water and the beacon turning a color she had never seen. Her lantern was issued for ferry survey work; its maker's plate identifies Crown survey works.

**Other witnesses:** a baker carries an ordinary sketch of Bellwether's square; a rope-maker recognizes the ferry cooperative's ticket; a teenager has a schoolbook stamped with the town's name. Their stories differ in minor experience, not in an artificially planted conspiracy. Let players choose whom to speak to. Do not require distress to be performed before believing them.

**Holt:** arrives with two assistants and asks whether anyone needs medical help. Her initial job is safety and a passenger record. She can inspect the boat, signal a launch and issue a temporary landing acknowledgment. If the characters explain the discrepancy, she agrees it deserves investigation. She cannot explain the engine from a fact she has not learned.

**Three accessible clues:**

1. H1, the printed ferry ticket, names Bellwether and the route.
2. The unlinked passenger sketch shows the town while the linked harbor map shows water.
3. The lantern's maker's plate and damaged ferry register point to Crown survey works and clerk Ivo Penn.

Give plain text when inspected. An optional DC 13 Investigation check can establish that the official map's alteration is fresh; failure does not hide its obvious difference from the sketch. An Insight check can help interpret a specific person's behavior, not prove all metaphysical facts or force trust.

**Who keeps the lantern?** Nessa is willing to lend it for an agreed return plan or accompany the expedition. Asking establishes a useful relationship. Taking it by force has a concrete effect on trust and her ability to navigate; it does not destroy all other instruments in the setting.

## Scene 3 — A disagreement worth resolving

A harbor clerk points out that Bellwether is invalid in the current arrival register. The clerk asks where to file the passengers, not whether they are people.

Offer the players a problem with several practical answers: record them as people currently in Brinewatch; secure Holt's signed temporary acknowledgment; invite Ivo to compare the records; or bypass official support and arrange independent shelter. Holt accepts a factual temporary record. The clerk can record uncertainty rather than fabricate a birthplace.

A civil explanation backed by the actual people and evidence resolves this without a roll. If the company asks for something disputed—such as immediate unrestricted access to the Crown works—state the concern and use a relevant check only for the uncertain part. A failed request leaves other resources available.

If violence is initiated, Holt tries to separate combatants and call a hearing. Use the Constable profile. Make withdrawal and surrender available under the adopted rules. Do not invent heavily armed reinforcements solely to punish the players. Record witnessed actions and any actual charges in Official Scrutiny.

## Scene 4 — The first shared table

Mara offers blankets and the Crooked Oar's common room for the night. If the company declines, Holt's emergency shelter or Orrin's warehouse can substitute. None requires the company to swear permanent loyalty.

Read only if they visit the Oar:

> Mara looks at the wet line of people in her doorway and moves a basket off the last free bench.
>
> “Boots there. Soup here. Explanations when you've got feeling back in your fingers.”

Invite each player to do one small thing at the meal: share food, mend something, ask a question, sit quietly with someone or exchange a joke. Nessa asks what a reliable return plan would need. Do not turn her request into an order to leave immediately.

Offer three known leads: Ivo's records, Orrin's pilot contacts and the Observatory's survey instruments. The company chooses what to pursue and what residents need overnight. Session 2 supplies a longer home-base adventure; a group with time can begin it now.

Holt or an independent relief collection can provide the one-time 30-gp-per-PC rescue grant. Record the source and amount once. It is a practical resource, not a test of allegiance. Mara's first-night help is unconditional; future supplies and accommodations need an arrangement.

## Close with a real decision

Ask the players what they want to do next. Finish with their plan and an observable image: the survey lantern casts a thin line across the table toward the bay. Do not announce that they have accepted a mission or formed a company unless they did.

Record passenger counts, injuries if any, ferry condition, lantern custody, evidence discovered, grant payment, shelter arrangements, promises, faction reactions, fictional time and the unresolved next choice. Start the global Storm Pressure at 0 on this first evening; its first forecast advance is 48 fictional hours later. The local ferry hazard does not change Storm Pressure.

## Adapting common surprises

| Player choice | Continue honestly |
|---|---|
| Refuses the rescue | Crew and dockworkers attempt it; resolve their actual means. Survivors can still request help later. Do not declare everyone dead as punishment |
| Solves everything with magic | Apply the spell, preserve the saved people and give the party the earned time |
| Leaves the city immediately | Follow the chosen route and tell them its known constraints. Refuge support can be delegated if someone agrees |
| Goes straight to the Crown | Provide the real public-access situation and a hearing/credential route; move later discoveries forward if earned |
| Accuses Holt or Nessa | Respond according to what each knows and wants; suspicion alone does not make the accusation true |
| Loses or destroys the lantern | Ivo and the Observatory provide the independent instrument/route path |
| Wants combat | Offer a conflict only when an actor has a reason to oppose them; it can be a later machinery encounter. Do not make the rescued passengers turn evil |

## Opening-session review

Before the next session, check whether each player understood the rescue objective, could propose a method beyond the examples, had a meaningful contribution and knows the next choice. Ask what felt confusing or slow. Tune later content from that feedback. This manuscript's readiness checks are proposed playtest criteria, not claims that a live session has passed.
